Music-guided walking app for older adults

Exercise people actually keep doing.

Andante uses the rhythm of music people enjoy to set the walking pace. Steps lock onto the beat without effort, and as the tempo gradually rises, their pace follows. Walks feel easier, and people come back for the next one.

No wearables. No extra kit. Just a phone and headphones.

How it works

The walk, the music and the practitioner, in sync.

  • The Person Walking

    Steps fall into time with the music's beat, without effort.

  • The App

    Tracks cadence and how closely steps match the beat in real time, and plays music at the right tempo to guide the pace.

  • The Dashboard

    Practitioners monitor progress remotely and adjust the programme.

The app

The right music for the right walk.

  • Simple enough to use at home.

    Built for one-tap use, so people can keep walking between visits.

  • A programme that keeps progressing.

    The tempo rises gradually and their steps follow, building to a challenge when they are ready, at a pace the practitioner sets.

  • Progress they can see.

    Results after every walk and simple graphs over the weeks, to keep them motivated.

The dashboard

See every walk, wherever it happens.

  • Know who needs you.

    Flags show who is inactive, cutting walks short, losing sync, or ready for a challenge, so your time goes where it matters.

  • See what actually happened.

    Review any walk in detail: cadence, sync, duration, which songs helped, plus your own notes.

  • Support your caseload remotely.

    Schedule walks and adjust each patient's programme without another visit.

Evidence

The science, now in your pocket.

Rhythm steadies walking.

Rhythmic Auditory Stimulation (RAS) gives the brain an external timing cue for each step. Hearing and movement are closely linked, so walking steadies without conscious effort.1

Music-based training has cut falls in older adults.

In a randomised trial of 134 older adults at increased risk of falling, a six-month music-based multitask training programme cut the fall rate by 54% and fall risk by 39%.2

Tempo matched to the person works best.

In a pilot study of healthy older adults, a tempo at or slightly above each person's own cadence worked best, while some fixed tempos made walking less stable.3

Music makes exercise feel easier.

A meta-analysis found that listening to music during exercise lowers perceived effort.4

  1. Thaut and Abiru 2010
  2. Trombetti et al. 2011
  3. Minino et al. 2021
  4. Terry et al. 2020
